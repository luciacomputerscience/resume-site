MootFinder: Find your Instagram Mutuals

Initially a Python script living on my PC, the MootFinder became a widely requested tool from my followers, so I created a web version to minimize the barrier to use. The app is fully clientside for security, and uses complex file parsing to decode Instagram data files. Users have full control of their data during the whole process, and recieve handy list of mutuals that they can later use to trim their following, tailor their "Favourites" feed, or organize collaborations.

Just Keep Swimming

Role: 2D Art Lead, Environment Artist, Programmer

Timeline: 2 weeks

Status: Prototype

Link:

Just Keep Swimming sits as my first 3D game and my first game in Godot.

As the 2D lead for a 3D game, I was responsible for producing 2D work that could easily be translated into 3D. Once the track was modeled, my role as an environment artist began: using earlier 2D concepts, I located open-source 3D assets, programmed shaders, and integrated all elements of the game into one cohesive, visually aesthetic scene. Finally, I was tasked with programming the shark. To create a racecar effect, the shark uses a VechicleBody3D node, which integrates a variety of unique car physics such as suspension alongside normal rigidbody physics. Afterwards, the sharks' speed to could be calculated to end the game when it went below a certain threshhold.

PaPause's Bistro

Role: Lead Programmer, Lead Artist

Timeline: 1 week

Status: Prototype

Link:

The watched burger never broils...

Keeping it simple with the theme of “Pause”, we make a quirky restaurant simulator based on the idea that “a watched pot never boils”; or in this case, that “a watched burger never broils.” In this game, I focused on scene switching and inventory management in Unity. To get the scene swapping effect without waiting on the Unity SceneLoader, I kept everything to one Unity scene and swapped the assets in the back to show the correct cooking station. For the art, I took direction from my teammate's experiences in a restaurant kitchen to create a clean but clearly used kitchen environment. In the face of time constraints, most of the food items were created with photobashing techniques.

MythMart

Role: Lead Artist

Timeline: 2 weeks

Status: Completed

Link:

Welcome to this interdimensional space of a store. Shop some?

This was my second game jam, taking place over the course of two weeks with "Branching Out" as our theme. We settled on the premise of an inter-dimensional grocery store tended by deities of various major ancient pantheons. After we settled on which gods would appear, I was tasked with painting all of the sets, worked closely with the game’s writer to convey each god’s character, and spent a lot of time reading ancient myths and looking at ancient art to accurately represent these figures. Due to the limited amount of characters, I snuck in references to other stories that didn’t show up through the background, particularly product labels.

Gambometer

Role: Lead Artist

Timeline: 3 days

Status: Prototype

Link:

Escape the scrap yard... By gambling.

Inspired by retrofuturism, comicbook, and synthwave aesthetics, I chose to keep the game’s art bright, punchy and lineless to grab attention and save time creating it. I was particularly inspired by the original TRON posters. Common elements across all art assets were sun-like rays, grids, screentones, flat colours, and gradients. Since the game was a rogue-like deckbuilder, I put most of my attention into designing the cards. There were three types of cards: red (attack), blue(shield), and green(healing/miscellaneous). Each card was themed primarily around the main colour of its category and was split into text and an image, similar to Pokemon cards. The image on the cards was designed with the intent to be bold and instantly communicate the effect of the card without words.
